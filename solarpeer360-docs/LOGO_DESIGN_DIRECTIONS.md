# SolarPeer 360 Logo Design Directions

**Document status:** **Approved** — Concept G (Market Sun Bolt) selected; production files generated  
**Brand direction:** Market Sun + Grid Green  
**Prepared for:** Founders, product designers, mobile engineers, web engineers, installers, and print vendors  
**Last updated:** 10 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the six proposed SolarPeer 360 logo directions and the requirements for turning the selected direction into a production-ready identity.

It should be used together with:

- `BRAND_GUIDE.md` for approved colours, typography, interface rules, and brand positioning.
- `PROJECT.md` for product scope and business rules.
- `TECH_STACK.md` for implementation technologies.

The logo must communicate trusted neighbourhood energy sharing, metered electricity, and payment-grade reliability. SolarPeer is not an open energy marketplace or a solar-equipment catalogue.

## 2. Brand Foundation

### Brand promise

> **Reliable power from the solar system next door.**

### Brand character

- Dependable and transparent
- Warm and locally relevant
- Simple for first-time digital users
- Operationally credible
- Modern without appearing futuristic or expensive

### Core audiences

- Low-income Nigerian SME merchants buying metered energy
- Solar owners acting as Energy CEOs
- Installers linking physical smart meters
- SolarPeer operations and support staff

### Required environments

The selected identity must work on:

1. Android launcher icons and splash screens
2. Buyer and seller mobile-app headers
3. Staff and installer dashboards
4. Public website and social media
5. Smart-meter labels and QR stickers
6. WhatsApp or printed receipts
7. Installer uniforms, shop signage, and presentations

### Colour rules

- Use no more than three brand colours in the primary logo.
- Keep the symbol recognizable in one colour.
- Use Energy Ink rather than white over Solar Amber.
- Do not use gradients in the master logo.
- Do not use bright fintech green as the dominant identity.
- Do not use colour alone to represent a meter or payment status.

## 4. Logo Concepts

### A. Solar Neighbourhood

**Idea:** A central Solar Amber sun connected to six Grid Green neighbour nodes. The six nodes reference a solar seller serving up to six nearby shops or tenants.

**Meaning:** Shared local generation, one trusted hub, connected buyers, and energy radiating through a neighbourhood.

**Wordmark:** `SolarPeer` in bold General Sans, with `360` as a controlled Market Rust accent.  
**Suggested supporting line:** `POWER SHARED LOCALLY`

**Strengths:**

- Explains the network model quickly.
- Friendly and understandable for non-technical users.
- Strong connection to SolarPeer's six-buyer pilot model.
- Converts naturally into an Android app icon.

**Risks to refine:**

- The network symbol must not resemble a virus or generic technology hub.
- Small nodes must remain visible on meter labels and favicons.
- The symbol should remain balanced if the six-buyer limit changes later.

### B. SP Energy Circuit

**Idea:** Interlocking `S` and `P` letterforms form a compact electrical path.

**Meaning:** SolarPeer technology, controlled energy routing, and a connected smart-meter system.

**Wordmark:** Uppercase `SOLARPEER` with a compact `ENERGY 360` descriptor.  
**Suggested type direction:** Space Grotesk or a custom geometric sans serif.

**Strengths:**

- Distinctive monogram for Android and hardware.
- Scales well at small sizes.
- Feels like a modern energy-technology company.

**Risks to refine:**

- The letters must remain readable and not appear like `CP`, a plug, or a payment-card logo.
- It communicates technology more strongly than community.

### C. Shared Roof

**Idea:** Two neighbouring market roofs connected beneath a rising Solar Amber sun.

**Meaning:** Real shops, next-door power, shared infrastructure, and local economic activity.

**Wordmark:** `SolarPeer` with the supporting line `ENERGY NEXT DOOR · 360`.  
**Suggested type direction:** Source Sans 3.

**Strengths:**

- Human and locally understandable.
- Closely matches the customer-facing promise.
- Suitable for field materials and merchant onboarding.

**Risks to refine:**

- May look like a housing, roofing, or property company.
- The roof detail can become unclear at favicon size.
- Must avoid looking informal or charity-led.

### D. Meter Pulse

**Idea:** A precision meter dial containing a Solar Amber power indicator and `360` detail.

**Meaning:** Every kilowatt-hour is measured, controlled, and visible.

**Wordmark:** Strong uppercase `SOLARPEER` with `MEASURED POWER`.  
**Suggested type direction:** IBM Plex Sans Condensed.

**Strengths:**

- Communicates metering and operational control immediately.
- Strong on physical smart meters and installer materials.
- Feels trustworthy and infrastructure-oriented.

**Risks to refine:**

- Could appear like a vehicle gauge, utility company, or equipment manufacturer.
- Less explicit about neighbour-to-neighbour sharing.
- Small tick marks may disappear in reduced sizes.

### E. Power Loop 360

**Idea:** A continuous circular loop represents energy moving from seller to buyer, with the loop also suggesting `360` and controlled exchange.

**Meaning:** Renewable circulation, reusable prepaid capacity, and a complete energy transaction.

**Wordmark:** Modern mixed-case `SolarPeer 360`.  
**Suggested type direction:** Manrope.

**Strengths:**

- Simple and highly scalable.
- Works well for app icons and motion graphics.
- Suggests energy exchange without showing complex hardware.

**Risks to refine:**

- Circular energy marks are common and may be difficult to protect.
- Could resemble recycling, synchronization, or charging brands.
- Requires a distinctive internal shape or cut.

### F. Energy CEO Wordmark

**Idea:** A confident custom SolarPeer wordmark with an integrated sun or power detail and a strong `360` signature.

**Meaning:** A scalable technology company that helps local solar owners become energy businesses.

**Wordmark:** Custom bold lettering based on Archivo or General Sans.  
**Suggested supporting line:** `LOCAL POWER. CLEAR VALUE.`

**Strengths:**

- Professional and flexible across investor, product, and commercial use.
- Can become highly distinctive through custom lettering.
- Avoids dependence on a complicated illustrative symbol.

**Risks to refine:**

- A wordmark alone may be weak at Android launcher size.
- It needs a strong standalone monogram or initial for meter and app use.
- It communicates the operating model less directly.

### G. Market Sun Bolt — **SELECTED**

**Idea:** A ~70%-round sun built from short, thick, irregular flame rays, opening at the base, with a bold lightning bolt driven vertically through its centre. The bolt does not sit on top of the sun — it is traced out of it, so a clean gap separates the two wherever they cross and the bolt reads as genuinely passing through.

**Meaning:** Solar generation (the flame sun) converted into delivered, metered electricity (the bolt). The open base is where the energy leaves the hub and reaches the buyer.

**Wordmark:** `SolarPeer 360` in bold Grid Green, set below the mark, with the supporting line `POWER SHARED LOCALLY` in tracked Solar Amber capitals.

**Strengths:**

- The bolt is unmistakably electricity — it reads at a glance to a non-technical merchant.
- The traced channel means the mark survives as a single flat colour: bolt and sun stay separate even in one-colour print and on thermal receipts.
- The open base gives the mark direction; it is not another closed circular badge.
- Sun + bolt is a distinct pairing against Lumos, Arnergy, and fintech-green identities.
- The flame rays carry the Nigerian market warmth the brand position asks for.

**Risks accepted:**

- More energetic than the restrained "payment utility" tone argued for in `BRAND_GUIDE.md` §1. This is a deliberate founder decision, recorded here.
- The thin ring band and fine flame tips soften below roughly 24 px. Mitigated by the dedicated icon lockup, which uses a shortened bolt tail and a tighter crop.
- The sun-plus-bolt combination is common in generic solar clip art. The irregular ray rhythm, the 70% opening, the traced bolt channel, and the brand palette are what separate it — do not let a future redraw regularise the rays into even triangles.

### Construction spec

The mark is generated, not hand-drawn, so it can be rebuilt exactly.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Sun centre | `(256, 204)` in a `0 0 512 512` viewBox |
| Ray/core angular span | `153°` to `387°` (234°, leaving a ~126° opening centred on the base) |
| Outer ring radius | `100` |
| Inner ring radius (void) | `84` — band is **16 units, ~5% of the mark width** |
| Ray seat radius | `94` |
| Ray count | 18, with the four arc-end rays shortened to a `+22` stub |
| Ray lengths (cycled) | `46, 28, 54, 34, 24, 48, 30, 42, 50, 26` |
| Ray base half-angles (cycled) | `4.8, 3.4, 5.6, 3.8, 3.0, 5.2, 3.6, 4.4` degrees |
| Bolt (lockup) | `M278 104 L202 220 L250 220 L232 332 L310 198 L258 198 Z` |
| Bolt (icon, short tail) | `M278 108 L206 218 L250 218 L236 306 L306 200 L258 200 Z` |
| Trace channel | bolt path stroked at width `18` (9 units of clearance per side), round joins, knocked out of the sun |

The sun is drawn as a filled wedge, then a mask removes the central void and the traced bolt channel. The bolt is painted last, inside its own channel.

## 5. Evaluation Criteria

Rate each direction from 1 to 5 against the following criteria:

| Criterion | Evaluation question |
|---|---|
| Immediate meaning | Does it suggest reliable local energy or metering without explanation? |
| Trust | Would a Lagos merchant trust it on a meter, receipt, or payment screen? |
| App-icon strength | Is it recognizable at 48 px and without the wordmark? |
| Hardware legibility | Does it print clearly on a small meter label in one colour? |
| Distinctiveness | Is it meaningfully different from solar, utility, and fintech competitors? |
| Scalability | Can it support future products without depending on the six-buyer pilot limit? |
| Cultural fit | Does it feel warm, practical, and locally relevant without using stereotypes? |

The preferred concept should not be finalized from the desktop wordmark alone. Test it with at least three target users, one seller, and one installer using the app-icon and meter-label versions.

## 6. Recommended Validation Questions

Show each participant the marks without concept names and ask:

1. What type of company do you think this is?
2. Which mark would you trust most on an electricity meter?
3. Which icon would be easiest to find on your phone?
4. Which looks most like reliable power rather than a loan, bank, or solar shop?
5. What does the selected symbol mean to you?

Record the first answer before explaining SolarPeer. Avoid asking whether the participant merely “likes” the logo.

### File naming convention

Pattern: `solarpeer360-<lockup>[-<colourway>].svg`

- **lockup** — `logo-stacked` (primary), `logo-horizontal`, or `icon`
- **colourway** — omitted for full colour; otherwise `reverse` (for dark surfaces), `mono-green`, or `mono-white`

```text
solarpeer360-logo-stacked.svg
solarpeer360-logo-stacked-reverse.svg
solarpeer360-logo-horizontal.svg
solarpeer360-icon.svg
solarpeer360-icon-mono-green.svg
```

Raster exports append the pixel width: `png/solarpeer360-icon-512.png`.

Variants are named by **colourway**, not by background (`-light` / `-dark`), because the reverse lockup changes several colours at once rather than just inverting one. Any future asset must follow this pattern.

## 8. Technical Implementation Rules

- Use SVG for product and website interfaces whenever possible.
- Give meaningful inline SVG titles or accessible image labels.
- Do not embed the full wordmark inside the small Android launcher icon.
- Preserve a simple silhouette at 24 px.
- Keep the master SVG free of unnecessary masks, raster images, and external fonts.
- Convert custom wordmark lettering to vector outlines only in distribution copies; retain an editable master.
- Provide Android adaptive-icon safe-zone testing so important details are not cropped into circles or rounded squares.
- Test one-colour output on low-cost label printers and photocopiers.
- Verify colour contrast when a logo is used near buttons, balances, or alerts.

## 9. Usage Guardrails

Do not:

- Stretch, rotate, outline, or add shadows to the logo.
- Recolour the logo with unrelated fintech greens or generic gradients.
- place a detailed full-colour mark on a busy photograph without a controlled holding shape.
- Use the logo as a meter-status or payment-status indicator.
- Add `Energy CEO` to the corporate master logo; use it as a seller-role label.
- Claim a concept is trademark-safe before a proper Nigerian and international trademark search.

## 10. Decision Status

The approved **Market Sun + Grid Green** colour system remains fixed. **Concept G — Market Sun Bolt** is the selected identity as of 10 September 2026.

Concepts A–F remain in this document as the exploration record. None was carried forward. The route to G ran through three rounds:

1. Six concepts explored (A–F); none convincing enough to lock.
2. A circular seal with a central bolt was explored, then rejected in favour of a sun burst after the founder supplied a visual reference.
3. The reference was translated into brand colours and then corrected in four passes: bolt enlarged and centred, flames shortened and thickened, the flat half-sun baseline removed in favour of a ~70%-round open base, and finally the bolt traced out of the sun rather than overlaid on it, with the ring band thinned to ~5%.

Colour rule §3 ("no more than three brand colours in the primary logo") is satisfied: the mark uses Solar Amber and Grid Green only, with Energy Ink reserved for the horizontal lockup's wordmark.

**Still outstanding before the mark is used in the field:**

- Field validation with real users has **not** been run. Section 6's questions remain open — test with at least three merchants, one seller, and one installer using the icon and meter-label versions before printing hardware labels at volume.
- A Nigerian and international trademark search has **not** been performed. Do not claim the mark is protected (see §9).
- The wordmark is currently set in Helvetica/Arial as a stand-in for General Sans. Reset it in licensed General Sans and convert to outlines before print production.

### Rebuilding or adjusting the mark

The geometry is parametric — see the construction spec in §4G. Changing ray count, ray length, ring thickness, or the opening angle means regenerating all ten SVGs together so the family stays consistent. Never edit one variant in isolation.

Filenames follow the convention recorded in §7.
