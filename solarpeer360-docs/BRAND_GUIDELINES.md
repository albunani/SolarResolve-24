# Brand Guidelines

## Brand promise

SolarPeer 360 makes nearby solar energy measurable, understandable, and accessible. The brand should communicate useful power, neighbour-to-neighbour participation, and disciplined operating controls.

## Positioning

**For** shops, students, households, solar owners, installers, and community organisers in Nigeria, **SolarPeer 360** is a local energy-access service that enables measured prepaid solar sharing from a verified nearby provider. Unlike generator fuel or an unaffordable complete solar system, it is designed around metered energy, safe power limits, and local clusters.

This is a proposed positioning statement, not public copy.

## Personality

- Practical, not futuristic
- Hopeful, not promotional
- Local, not stereotypical
- Clear, not technical for its own sake
- Candid about pilot limitations
- Serious about safety and money

## Voice

Use short sentences and familiar examples. Explain technical terms at first use. Prefer “buy 4 kWh” over “receive an energy allocation”. Prefer “an installer checks your site” over “technical validation is performed”.

Do not claim guaranteed savings, continuous supply, verified performance, signed partnerships, or regulatory approval unless evidence is current and publishable.

## Name and tagline

- Product name: `SolarPeer 360`
- Current descriptive line: `Measured, prepaid solar energy between neighbours.`
- Current campaign proposition: `Reliable power from the solar system next door.`

The campaign line is memorable but can be read as an astronomy reference. Always pair it with a plain-language explanation on first exposure.

## Logo

- Preserve the approved clear space around the sun-and-lightning mark.
- Use the reverse horizontal logo on forest-green surfaces.
- Do not recolour individual logo elements outside approved files.
- Do not use the logo as evidence of certification or installer approval.
- Provide accessible link text such as “SolarPeer 360 home”; the image may be decorative inside that link.

## Colour and typography

The forest green signals stability and local infrastructure. Solar yellow signals energy and action. Cream reduces glare and keeps long pages approachable. Source Sans 3 is the primary typeface because it remains legible at small sizes and supports the site’s direct tone.

Exact tokens live in [Design system](DESIGN.md).

## Photography

Every field photograph should include location, date, activity, consent status, and an evidence classification. Avoid captions that imply performance results when the photo only proves attendance or installation activity.

## Terminology

Use consistently:

- `solar owner`, not host, prosumer, or generator
- `buyer`, when discussing the energy transaction
- `participant`, when discussing the pilot generally
- `community cluster`, for geographically viable groups
- `kWh`, for energy purchased
- `power limit`, for the simultaneous wattage allowed
